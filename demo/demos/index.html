Every demonstration here runs the real program. Nothing is a mock-up, a screenshot or a video: when you press a button, the runtime's own verifier runs and its output is printed below, unedited. All eight can be reproduced from a shell with the command each page prints.

Run all of it yourself, without us

Four of the eight are interactive and call this machine when you press a button — which is convenient, and worth nothing as evidence, because you are still taking our word for it. The other four print what a run produced. Either way, the point is that none of it needs our permission:

curl -O https://mint.arliwork.com/receipts.json
curl -O https://mint.arliwork.com/policy.json
curl -O https://mint.arliwork.com/endorsement.json
mint receipts --in receipts.json --policy policy.json --endorsement endorsement.json

That verifies every signature, walks the hash chain, re-applies the published rules to every recorded decision, checks the counter-signature, and prints the receipt where anything first disagrees. The log and the rules are the whole input; nothing is fetched from us at check time, and nothing about the check depends on the program that produced the log.

the files behind the demonstrations: /receipts.json · /policy.json · /endorsement.json · /evaluation.json · /measurements.json

what no demonstration here shows

A real counterparty. Every key on this site was generated on this machine, so the demonstrations prove the mechanism and say nothing about who is behind it. Nor do they show that the work done was the work wanted — only that what was recorded truthfully describes what was done, signably and checkably. Those two gaps are the ones worth arguing about, and they are stated here rather than left for a reader to find.
