The gate · 5 of 12

What this is: The rules engine deciding, live: reading passes, writing waits, the undescribable is refused.

Who it is for: The person who has to approve what an agent does.

What to look at: That an approval is bound to one specific action, not to 'the agent'.

demonstration 1 · live

The gate answers one question: what would this step do?

Every step goes through a gate before it touches anything. The gate does not look at what an element is called, and it does not take a model's word for it. It looks at what the element would do — read, write, or something nobody can describe — and from that comes allow, hold or refuse.

What the rules actually say

  
A navigation is a read, whatever it looks like — arriving somewhere changes nothing on
  the far side. Typing is a read; the commit, if there is one, is the submit. Anything that commits is
  held until an approval bound to that exact action is presented, and then released once. An
  element that matches no rule is refused even when an approval is offered, because an approval
  authorises a described action and there is nothing here to describe.

  
Try the fourth one with the switch on

  
It still refuses. That is the whole point: an approval is a permission for one
  described action, not a key to the front door. A gate that can be talked into anything is not a gate,
  it is a formality with a good reputation.




what this does not show
It does not show that the rules are good rules. They are one page of somebody's judgement about what should require approval, published so you can disagree with it. The mechanism is that they are a document and every verdict can be re-derived from it — not that this particular document is right.
