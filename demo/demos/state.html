demonstration 8

What it hands to a model, and what it refuses to hand over.

Most of the cost and most of the mistakes in this kind of agent come from what gets sent to the model. So the runtime sends a small typed summary of a page instead of the page, and here is what that comes to on three real repositories.

Reading a page takes single-digit milliseconds. The summary is around 28–30 kB. The same page serialised as a DOM tree is around a megabyte and a few thousand nodes, and that is what most agents in this category send — every time, at every step.

Why this is a correctness argument, not a cost argument

Two reasons, and neither is about money. First, a model given a megabyte of markup answers about the markup: it has more to look at and less to be sure of. Second, and worse, a step compiled against raw markup breaks when the markup changes — and markup changes on every page view, for reasons that have nothing to do with the task. A step compiled against a shape survives a deployment of the site it was learned on. That is the difference between a recording and a program.

what this does not show

It does not show that the summary is sufficient for every task. It carries what the runtime knows how to act on: roles, names, whether something has a destination. A task that depends on a number buried in a table cell, or on a colour, or on text that is not a name, is not served by this summary — and the runtime refuses such a step rather than guessing at it.
