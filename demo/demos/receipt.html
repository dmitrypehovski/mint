what this proves, and what it does not

A receipt proves that this record was issued by the holder of the stated key and has not been altered since. It does not prove the action was correct, that its inputs were true, that a person approved it, or that the world changed the way the record describes.

The two things a receipt is not allowed to leave out

It carries the observations the runtime acted on — the tag, the role, whether there was a link, whether an approval was presented — and not merely its own conclusion. That is what lets someone else re-apply the rules and disagree. And it carries its own limit, in the claim above, so that a reader who only ever sees one receipt cannot mistake it for a statement about whether the work was correct.

the file: /receipts.json — the whole log, as published. This page reads that same file.

what this does not show

It does not show that the elements named in the receipt were the ones on the page. The runtime attests to what it observed, not to the page it observed it on. Attesting to the environment needs a third party who can see both, and this does not have one.
