The gap a chain cannot close

A chain proves nothing was edited. It cannot prove nothing was removed.

Every receipt is signed, and each one commits to the hash of the one before it, so editing a receipt breaks the chain at exactly that point. But whoever holds the log can also simply drop the last ten receipts and hand you a shorter log in which every link verifies perfectly. Nothing inside a chain can tell you that the chain is the whole chain — that question is about the log, not about anything in it.

A second party answers it, and the answer takes two small objects.

The checkpoint

The runtime signs the head of its own log: how many receipts there are, which sequence number the last one carries, and the hash it ends with.

The countersignature

A different key signs exactly that head, and the witness keeps what it saw. A log presented later with fewer receipts, or with a different receipt at a sequence number it already used, is then a contradiction rather than a matter of opinion.

The ledger

The witness's own file of sightings, written by the witness and not by the runtime — the thing a runtime cannot edit and so the thing that makes a shrink visible.

Try to get past it

Three fixed cases run the real verifier: nothing is built from what you send, and none of it writes anything. The output below is the runtime's own.

Press a button.






  What this does not prove. On this site the witness runs on the same machine as the runtime:
  both keys are held here, so what you are watching is the mechanism working, not a reason to trust
  it. A witness worth the name is somebody who does not hold the log. And a witness says nothing about
  the world — it countersigns what was recorded, including a lie, and can only say that the record did
  not change afterwards.




From a shell


Each of these is one command and no service. A stranger needs the three files and this
binary, nothing else.


mint checkpoint      --receipts receipts.json --out checkpoint.json    # the holder of the log
mint witness         --checkpoint checkpoint.json --ledger ledger.json  # the witness, with its own key
mint verify-witness  --receipts receipts.json --checkpoint checkpoint.json --witness witness.json




  
The same three commands produced checkpoint.json,
  witness.json and witness-ledger.json,
  which are served here as they were written.
